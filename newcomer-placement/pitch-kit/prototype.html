    Step 3 · Coordinator with interpreter · ~10 min

    Schooling-history interview

    The same questions for every student, based on Massachusetts SLIFE guidance. Each question has a script to read aloud in the home language.

    Q1How many years did you go to school?Konbyen ane ou te al lekòl?8 years
    Q2Were there times you could not go to school? For how long?Èske te gen yon moman ou pa t ka al lekòl? Konbyen tan?Yes · 14 months (2023–2024)
    Q3What was your last school like?Kijan dènye lekòl ou te ye?Public · urban · Port-au-Prince
    Q4Which languages can you read and write?Nan ki lang ou ka li ak ekri?Haitian Creole, some French

    What the form does not ask

    No questions about immigration status, visas, or how the family arrived. The form has no fields for them.

    Summary so far

    • 8 years of schooling, 14-month interruption
    • Literate in Haitian Creole; some French
    • Last grade completed: roughly 8th

    Step 4 · Student view · ~10 min

    Home-language reading

    A short passage in Haitian Creole at one of three levels. The English line under each part is for staff only.

    Lekti · Pasaj 2 sou 3
    Chak maten, Jozèf leve bonè pou l ede manman l nan mache a. Li pote panyen fwi yo sou tèt li. Apre sa, li mache al lekòl. Jozèf renmen matematik paske li ka konte lajan kliyan yo byen vit.

    Staff: Every morning Joseph gets up early to help his mother at the market… He likes math because he can count customers' money quickly.

    Poukisa Jozèf renmen matematik?

    Staff: Why does Joseph like math?

    Sample passage and translation are drafts for the prototype.

    Step 5 · Student view · ~20 min, adaptive

    Visual math screener

    Questions use pictures, numbers and audio, so a student's math isn't hidden behind English. It stops when the starting band is clear.

    Matematik · 9 / ~20

    Ki nimewo ki pi gwo?

    Staff: "Which number is greater?" Skill: comparing integers (grade 6–7).
