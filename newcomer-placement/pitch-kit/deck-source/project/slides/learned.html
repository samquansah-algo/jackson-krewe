What customer discovery taught us

Starting points vary widely.

Some students lack basic number sense; others arrive ready to leave ESL quickly. Math can be the main barrier, not English.

Self-paced tools fail at home.

i-Ready and Lexia go unfinished when students work alone outside school. So Waypoint runs in school, with an adult.

One checkbox decides too much.

Marking a home language other than English leads to ESL placement with no academic check, and families often don't know why.

Source: Mom Test interview with a South Carolina ESOL and math educator (2026). Massachusetts interviews are next.

Key learning: a teacher we interviewed said i-Ready and Lexia fail when students use them alone at home, so Waypoint runs in school with an adult. This is one interview; we treat it as a hypothesis to confirm in Massachusetts.
